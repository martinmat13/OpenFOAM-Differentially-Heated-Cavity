### 1. Verification vs. Validation: Solving the Equations Right

In our previous articles, we established the baseline steady-state setup, explored RANS turbulence modeling with $k-\omega$ SST, and simulated time-accurate plume shedding with the PIMPLE algorithm. However, an essential question remains unanswered:

> **How much of our computed heat transfer is real physical flow, and how much is numerical discretization error from our grid?**

In computational fluid dynamics (CFD), the boundary between *Verification* and *Validation* (V&V) defines professional engineering practice:
- **Verification:** *"Are we solving the mathematical equations right?"* (Quantifying numerical algorithm errors, code bugs, and grid discretization error).
- **Validation:** *"Are we solving the right equations?"* (Quantifying how closely the physical model reflects experimental reality).

Grid refinement helps estimate numerical error before comparing with literature. A three-grid calculation does not automatically establish mesh independence: the observed convergence must meet the method's assumptions and pass an asymptotic-range check. The results in this study do not pass that check for mean Nusselt number.

---

### 2. Richardson Extrapolation & The Grid Convergence Index (GCI)

Rather than arbitrarily increasing cell counts and visually guessing when fields stop changing, the **ASME V&V 20-2009 Standard** (*Standard for Verification and Validation in Computational Fluid Dynamics and Heat Transfer*), based on the foundational work of **P. J. Roache (1998)** and **Celik et al. (2008)**, provides a rigorous mathematical framework.

#### 2.1 The Concept of Richardson Extrapolation
For any discrete numerical scheme, Taylor series truncation error dictates that a computed quantity $f(h)$ at characteristic cell size $h$ approaches the exact continuum solution $f_{exact}$ according to:

`$f(h) = f_{exact} + C_1 h^p + C_2 h^{p+1} + \dots$`

When the mesh is sufficiently refined to enter the **asymptotic range of convergence**, higher-order terms become negligible:

`$f(h) \approx f_{exact} + C h^p$`

Richardson Extrapolation uses solutions from multiple systematically refined grids to mathematically eliminate the leading error term `$C h^p$`, yielding the continuum zero-spacing estimate `$f_{ext}$` without needing an infinitely fine mesh.

#### 2.2 Core Concepts & Mathematical Formulation

Let $f_1, f_2, f_3$ denote discrete solutions evaluated on fine, medium, and coarse meshes with a constant grid refinement ratio $r = h_3/h_2 = h_2/h_1 = 2.0$:

1. **Step Differences:**
   `$\epsilon_{21} = f_2 - f_1$`, `$\epsilon_{32} = f_3 - f_2$`
   - If `$\epsilon_{32}/\epsilon_{21} > 0$`, convergence is **monotonic**.
   - If `$\epsilon_{32}/\epsilon_{21} < 0$`, convergence is **oscillatory**.

2. **Observed Apparent Order of Convergence ($p$):**
   `$p = \ln|\epsilon_{32} / \epsilon_{21}| / \ln(r)$`
   The apparent order indicates how rapidly truncation error drops with grid refinement. It reflects the real, observed spatial accuracy of the coupled solver.

3. **Richardson Extrapolated Value ($f_{ext}$):**
   `$f_{ext}^{21} = f_1 + (f_1 - f_2) / (r^p - 1)$`
   This represents the predicted solution at zero cell size ($h \to 0$).

4. **Grid Convergence Index ($GCI$):**
   The GCI uses a safety factor ($F_s = 1.25$ for a three-grid study) to report a discretization-error estimate under the method's convergence assumptions. It is not a statistical 95% confidence interval:
   - Fine Grid Uncertainty: `$GCI_{fine}^{21} = (F_s \cdot e_a^{21}) / (r^p - 1)$`
   - Medium Grid Uncertainty: `$GCI_{medium}^{32} = (F_s \cdot e_a^{32} \cdot r^p) / (r^p - 1)$`
   where `$e_a^{21} = |(f_1 - f_2) / f_1| \times 100\%$` is the relative step change.

5. **Asymptotic Range Verification ($\chi$):**
   `$\chi = GCI_{32} / (r^p \cdot GCI_{21}) \approx 1.0$`
   When $\chi \approx 1.0$, the results are consistent with the asymptotic range. This is a necessary diagnostic for interpreting the extrapolation, not a guarantee of correctness.

#### 4.1 How Each Verification Metric is Calculated & Its Relevance

To understand why verification is the cornerstone of credible computational fluid dynamics, here is how each column in the table is mathematically computed and what it physically signifies:

1. **Discrete Grid Solutions ($f_3, f_2, f_1$):**
   - **How it is obtained:** Raw simulation outputs evaluated on systematically refined meshes: Coarse ($f_3$, $h_3 = 0.020$ m), Medium ($f_2$, $h_2 = 0.010$ m), and Fine ($f_1$, $h_1 = 0.005$ m).
   - **Relevance:** Establishes the three discrete data points required to solve for the three unknowns in the truncation error expansion: continuum solution ($f_{ext}$), error coefficient ($C$), and apparent order ($p$).

2. **Approximate Relative Error ($e_a^{21}$):**
   - **Formula:** `$e_a^{21} = |(f_1 - f_2) / f_1| \times 100\%$`
   - **Relevance:** Measures the step-to-step percentage change from the medium to the fine mesh. A small value alone does not prove that further refinement is negligible or that the solutions are asymptotic.

3. **Observed Apparent Order of Convergence ($p$):**
   - **Formula:** `$p = \ln|(f_3 - f_2) / (f_2 - f_1)| / \ln(r)$`
   - **Relevance:** Provides an apparent rate for the sampled grid triplet when its values are suitable for the Richardson model. It is not by itself proof of asymptotic convergence; in this dataset the apparent order and asymptotic ratio must be interpreted together.

4. **Richardson Extrapolated Continuum Solution ($f_{ext}$):**
   - **Formula:** `$f_{ext} = f_1 + (f_1 - f_2) / (r^p - 1)$`
   - **Relevance:** Estimates the zero-spacing limit if the leading-order error model is valid. Treat the reported extrapolation as a conditional estimate, not an exact continuum solution.

5. **Fine Grid Convergence Index ($GCI_{fine}$):**
   - **Formula:** `$GCI_{fine} = (F_s \cdot e_a^{21}) / (r^p - 1)$` (with ASME safety factor $F_s = 1.25$ for 3-grid studies).
   - **Relevance:** The GCI provides a standardized discretization-error estimate when the convergence assumptions are supported. It is not a statistical confidence interval. Here $\chi=15.989$ for $\overline{Nu}$, so $0.16\%$ is not a defensible uncertainty bound.

6. **Asymptotic Ratio (\chi):**
   - **Formula:** `$\chi = GCI_{32} / (r^p \cdot GCI_{21})$`
   - **Relevance:** Checks whether the grid triplet is consistent with the asymptotic range. A value far from one, as in the Nusselt result here, prevents an asymptotic-convergence claim.

#### 4.2 In-Depth Analysis of the Verification Results

The current grid sequence is valuable as a diagnostic exercise, not as evidence that the cavity solution is grid independent:

1. **Mean Nusselt number:** Values increase monotonically from 41.1717 to 58.4341 to 59.5341, but $\chi=15.989$ fails the asymptotic-range check. The reported $\overline{Nu}_{ext}=59.6090$ and $GCI_{fine}=0.16\%$ are conditional script outputs, not verified uncertainty bounds.
2. **Center temperature:** The values remain close to 300 K, as expected from the symmetric wall temperatures. This agreement for a smooth central quantity does not establish convergence of wall gradients or velocity peaks.
3. **Horizontal velocity:** The values 0.0451, 0.0226, and 0.0278 m/s are non-monotonic. The reported apparent order and GCI should not be interpreted as proof of first-order asymptotic accuracy.
4. **Vertical velocity:** The values rise from 0.0854 to 0.1285 to 0.1935 m/s, and the nominal fine-grid GCI is 584.87%. The large variation makes clear that the peak is mesh-sensitive in this sequence. A boundary-layer explanation is plausible but has not been isolated by these calculations.

For a defensible uncertainty study, first audit the physical setup and quantity extraction, then refine the grids and use a method appropriate to the observed convergence behavior. Do not infer engineering design accuracy from the present table.

### 5. Key Engineering Conclusions

1. **Do not claim mesh independence from this run.** The Nusselt asymptotic ratio is $\chi=15.989$, and the velocity metrics are strongly grid-sensitive or non-monotonic.
2. **Use the table to learn the diagnostics.** It shows why small fine/medium changes and apparent orders are not sufficient on their own.
3. **Next step:** audit the case properties and extraction, add a suitable graded near-wall mesh if required, and rerun with convergence histories and a justified uncertainty analysis.
