### 1. Verification vs. Validation: Solving the Equations Right

In our previous articles, we established the baseline steady-state setup, explored RANS turbulence modeling with $k-\omega$ SST, and simulated time-accurate plume shedding with the PIMPLE algorithm. However, an essential question remains unanswered:

> **How much of our computed heat transfer is real physical flow, and how much is numerical discretization error from our grid?**

In computational fluid dynamics (CFD), the boundary between *Verification* and *Validation* (V&V) defines professional engineering practice:
- **Verification:** *"Are we solving the mathematical equations right?"* (Quantifying numerical algorithm errors, code bugs, and grid discretization error).
- **Validation:** *"Are we solving the right equations?"* (Quantifying how closely the physical model reflects experimental reality).

Before claiming that a simulation validates against benchmark literature (such as De Vahl Davis), we must first verify that our solution is **mesh-independent**. Without a formal mesh convergence study, a CFD result is merely a point estimate of unknown numerical uncertainty.

In this study, we implement the **ASME V&V 20-2009 Standard** (*Standard for Verification and Validation in Computational Fluid Dynamics and Heat Transfer*) based on the foundational work of **P. J. Roache (1998)** and **Celik et al. (2008)**.

### 2. The ASME V&V 20 Methodology: Richardson Extrapolation & GCI

The **Grid Convergence Index (GCI)** provides an objective, standardized method to determine the discretization error band on any computed quantity of interest ($f$). Rather than arbitrarily doubling cell counts and visually comparing plots, the GCI uses **Richardson Extrapolation** across three systematically refined meshes.

#### 2.2 Mathematical Formulation

Let $f_1, f_2, f_3$ denote the discrete numerical solutions on the fine, medium, and coarse grids, respectively.

1. **Step Differences:**
   `$\epsilon_{21} = f_2 - f_1$`, `$\epsilon_{32} = f_3 - f_2$`
   If `$\epsilon_{32}/\epsilon_{21} > 0$`, the numerical convergence is monotonic. If negative, it is oscillatory.

2. **Observed Apparent Order of Convergence ($p$):**
   For a constant refinement ratio $r = 2.0$:
   `$p = \ln|\epsilon_{32} / \epsilon_{21}| / \ln(r)$`

3. **Richardson Extrapolation (Continuum Zero-Spacing Solution):**
   `$f_{ext}^{21} = f_1 + (f_1 - f_2) / (r^p - 1)$`

4. **Relative Discretization Errors:**
   - Approximate Relative Error: `$e_a^{21} = |(f_1 - f_2) / f_1| \times 100\%$`
   - Extrapolated Relative Error: `$e_{ext}^{21} = |(f_{ext}^{21} - f_1) / f_{ext}^{21}| \times 100\%$`

5. **Grid Convergence Index ($GCI$):**
   Following ASME guidelines with a safety factor $F_s = 1.25$ for three-grid studies:
   `$GCI_{fine}^{21} = (F_s \cdot e_a^{21}) / (r^p - 1)$`
   `$GCI_{medium}^{32} = (F_s \cdot e_a^{32} \cdot r^p) / (r^p - 1)$`

6. **Asymptotic Convergence Criterion:**
   `$\chi = GCI_{32} / (r^p \cdot GCI_{21}) \approx 1.0$`
   When the asymptotic ratio $\chi$ approaches unity, the discrete solutions are verified to reside within the asymptotic convergence range where higher-order truncation terms become negligible.

**Why does $v_{max}$ increase as the grid is refined?**
In high-Rayleigh natural convection ($Ra \approx 2 \times 10^9$), the thermal and momentum boundary layer thickness scales as:
`$\delta \sim L \cdot Ra^{-1/4} \approx 1.0 \cdot (2.06 \times 10^9)^{-1/4} \approx 4.7$` mm

- **On Grid 3 (Coarse):** The cell width is $\Delta x = 20$ mm. Because $\Delta x > \delta$, the entire buoyant updraft is trapped inside a single cell! First-order numerical diffusion artificially broadens and slows the velocity peak ($v_{max} = 0.085$ m/s).
- **On Grid 2 (Medium):** With $\Delta x = 10$ mm, the boundary layer spans ~0.5 cells. The velocity jet sharpens ($v_{max} = 0.128$ m/s).
- **On Grid 1 (Fine):** With $\Delta x = 5$ mm, the cell size finally approaches the physical boundary layer scale, resolving the sharp buoyant jet near $x = 0.01$ m ($v_{max} = 0.193$ m/s).

This demonstrates why uniform Cartesian grids are suboptimal for high-Rayleigh boundary layers and why graded meshes (clustering cells near the wall to achieve $y^+ < 1$ and $\ge 10$ cells within $\delta$) are essential for high-fidelity velocity captures.

### 6. Apparent Order ($p$) vs. Formal Theoretical Order ($p_{formal}$)

In theoretical numerical analysis, the spatial discretization order of the scheme is known as the **formal order** ($p_{formal} = 1$ for first-order upwind, $p_{formal} = 2$ for central differencing or linearUpwind). 

In practical CFD, however, the **observed apparent order** ($p$) often deviates from $p_{formal}$:
1. **Grid Resolution Regime:** If the mesh is not yet within the asymptotic regime for a localized feature (such as the 4.7 mm boundary layer on a 20 mm grid), the observed order will reflect pre-asymptotic behavior ($p < 1$).
2. **Global Integrated Quantities:** For integral metrics like $\overline{Nu}$, cancellation of positive and negative errors across the domain can produce apparent super-convergence ($p \approx 3.97$), which collapses towards the true theoretical order as $h \to 0$.
3. **Boundary Condition Formulation:** Wall gradient approximations (such as one-sided first-order differences at boundary faces) can reduce an interior second-order scheme to first-order near boundaries.

### 7. Engineering Takeaways & Best Practices

1. **Always Report GCI with Confidence Intervals:**  
   Never report a single CFD result as absolute truth. Reporting $\overline{Nu} = 59.53$ with an ASME $GCI_{fine} = \pm 0.16\%$ demonstrates professional verification rigor.
2. **Medium (100x100) vs Fine (200x200) Efficiency Tradeoff:**  
   The Medium grid required only **35 seconds** of compute time to achieve within **1.85%** of the fine grid solution (which took **169 seconds**). For engineering parameter studies, the Medium grid represents the optimal cost-accuracy Pareto point.
3. **Boundary Layer Resolution is Paramount:**  
   Accurately capturing velocity peaks in buoyancy-driven flows requires boundary layer grading rather than pure uniform refinement. Estimating $\delta \sim L \cdot Ra^{-1/4}$ before creating `blockMeshDict` is crucial.
